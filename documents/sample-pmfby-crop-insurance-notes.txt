PRADHAN MANTRI FASAL BIMA YOJANA (PMFBY) — OVERVIEW

PMFBY is the Government of India's crop insurance scheme. Its purpose is to
give farmers financial support when a notified crop fails because of natural
causes that were outside the farmer's control.

WHAT IT IS MEANT TO COVER

Losses from natural fire and lightning, storms, hail, cyclone, flood,
inundation, landslide, drought and dry spells, and pests and disease, for crops
that have been notified for that season in that area. Certain localised risks
and post-harvest losses are also covered under the conditions the guidelines
set out.

WHAT IT DOES NOT COVER

Losses caused by war, nuclear risks, malicious damage, and other risks the
guidelines expressly exclude. Damage the farmer could have prevented through
normal care is generally not covered.

WHO CAN ENROL

Farmers growing a notified crop in a notified area may enrol. This includes
both landowner farmers and, subject to the documentation rules in the
guidelines, sharecroppers and tenant farmers. Enrolment is voluntary.

DOCUMENTS USUALLY ASKED FOR

Aadhaar number. Bank account details, because claims are paid directly into the
account. Land records such as the khasra or khatauni. For tenant farmers, a
valid land-tenancy agreement or the equivalent document accepted in that state.
A sowing declaration for the season.

HOW TO ENROL

Through a bank branch where the farmer holds a Kisan Credit Card or an account.
Through a PACS. Through a Common Service Centre. Or directly on the national
crop insurance portal.

REPORTING A CROP LOSS

The guidelines require the farmer to report the loss quickly after it happens.
The report can be made through the scheme's toll-free helpline, through the
crop insurance app, or through the bank, the insurance company or the local
agriculture department office.

Reporting late is the most common reason a claim is rejected, so a farmer who
has suffered a loss should report first and gather paperwork afterwards.

Because the reporting window, the premium share and the notified crop list are
revised from season to season, a farmer should confirm the current dates with
their PACS, their bank branch, or the district agriculture office before
relying on any of them.
