WHAT A PRIMARY AGRICULTURAL CREDIT SOCIETY (PACS) IS

A Primary Agricultural Credit Society, usually shortened to PACS, is the
village-level unit of India's three-tier short-term cooperative credit
structure. Above it sits the District Central Cooperative Bank, and above that
the State Cooperative Bank. A PACS is owned by its members, who are farmers and
rural households from the villages it serves.

Because it is member-owned, a PACS is not a branch of a bank taking orders from
a head office. Its members elect a managing committee, and that committee is
answerable to the general body of members.

COMMON SERVICES A PACS PROVIDES

Short-term crop loans for seeds, fertiliser and other cultivation costs.
Medium-term loans for equipment, livestock and land improvement.
Supply of farm inputs such as fertiliser, seeds and pesticides.
Storage and warehousing facilities for harvested produce.
Deposit and savings accounts for members.
Distribution point for several government schemes.

Under the computerisation programme run by the Ministry of Cooperation, many
PACS have been given expanded roles, which can include operating as a common
service centre, a fair price shop, or a point for utility bill collection.

MEMBERSHIP IN GENERAL TERMS

Membership is normally open to adults residing in the society's area of
operation who meet the conditions written in that society's by-laws. A member
buys at least one share, pays any admission fee the by-laws set, and gains the
right to vote in the general body.

Important: the exact eligibility conditions, share value, and admission
procedure are set by each society's own by-laws and by the cooperative act of
the state concerned. They are not uniform across India. Always check the
by-laws of the specific society and the applicable state act.

RIGHTS A MEMBER GENERALLY HOLDS

The right to attend and vote at general body meetings.
The right to contest elections to the managing committee, subject to the
disqualifications listed in the by-laws.
The right to inspect certain records of the society, as permitted by the act.
The right to receive a share of any dividend the society declares.
The right to raise a dispute through the mechanism the act provides.

WHERE MEMBERS SHOULD GO WITH QUESTIONS

The secretary of the society for day-to-day matters. The Registrar of
Cooperative Societies for the district, for disputes, elections, audits and
complaints that the society itself has not resolved.
